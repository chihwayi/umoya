# Southern Africa Advanced Private Hospital Readiness Roadmap (S274+)

**Created:** 2026-08-26. Requested explicitly: research what top-tier private hospitals/clinics in Zimbabwe and Southern Africa actually need, and check whether Umoya covers it.

## Method

1. Read the full current README feature set (Clinical Capabilities, Hospital and Specialty Modules, SADC/Africa-Specific Modules, HIV Centre of Excellence) — this is already an exceptionally broad clinical feature set: ICU with APACHE II/SOFA scoring, NICU with HIE/cooling protocols, cardiac cath lab, dialysis/CRRT, aviation medicine, HBOT, prosthetics, 20+ specialty modules, and a genuinely deep HIV Centre of Excellence.
2. Web research on real private hospital groups operating in the target market:
   - Zimbabwe: [List of hospitals in Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hospitals_in_Zimbabwe), [Best hospitals in Zimbabwe](https://www.bestcosmetichospitals.com/blog/top-20-best-hospitals-in-zimbabwe/) — Avenues Clinic, Baines Intercare, Mater Dei (Bulawayo), St Anne's.
   - Southern Africa private groups: [Mediclinic Southern Africa](https://www.mediclinic.co.za/en/corporate/about-mediclinic-southern-africa.html) (50 hospitals, 8,945+ beds across SA/Namibia), Netcare, Life Healthcare — [South Africa Healthcare Sector Report](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/south-africa-healthcare-sector-report-115900841.html).
   - Netcare's proprietary EMR (**CareOn**) and its flagship AI feature: an ICU deterioration-prediction model that flags decline up to **10 hours in advance** — a useful concrete benchmark for what "advanced" ICU AI means at this tier of hospital.
   - [COHSASA](https://cohsasa.co.za/) (Council for Health Service Accreditation of Southern Africa) — the only ISQua-recognized healthcare accreditation body operating across Zimbabwe, South Africa, Botswana, Lesotho, Namibia, Zambia, and more. This is the actual accreditation standard private hospitals in this exact market pursue (as opposed to JCI, which is more relevant to the Gulf/Asia medical-tourism market). Their standards require, as baseline: governance and leadership, **risk management**, infection prevention and control (already strong in Umoya), and — critically — an **incident reporting and management** module (their own reference system is called PatSIS), plus ongoing quality-improvement tracking via their **CoQIS** digital system.
3. Cross-checked every hypothesis against the actual codebase (`grep` across `database-provisioning.service.ts` and relevant services) before listing it as a gap — same discipline as the rest of this engagement. Nothing below is speculative; each was verified absent or present-but-narrow in the current schema.

## What's already excellent (do not rebuild)

Umoya's clinical depth already matches or exceeds what a private hospital group's proprietary system typically covers: ICU (APACHE II/SOFA, ventilator management, vasopressor titration), NICU (HIE grading, cooling protocol, targeted echo), cardiac cath lab with AI risk stratification, dialysis/CRRT, a full HIV Centre of Excellence with 95-95-95 cascade + VigiBase pharmacovigilance, aviation medicine to CAAZ/ICAO standard, and 20+ additional specialty modules. The gaps below are specifically about **hospital-operations and accreditation infrastructure** that sits alongside clinical care — the layer COHSASA-style accreditation actually audits — not clinical depth, which is not lacking.

## Part 1 — Findings (G1–G6)

| # | Finding | Evidence | Severity |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | **No general-purpose patient-safety incident reporting & RCA system.** The only "incident"/"near-miss" tracking in the entire schema is `oncology_near_miss_events` (chemotherapy-specific) and `workplace_incidents` (OEM employer injury tracking). There is nothing for falls, medication errors, wrong-site/wrong-patient events, specimen mislabeling, retained foreign objects, or any other hospital-wide patient-safety event — the exact category COHSASA's own reference system (PatSIS) exists to cover, and a baseline requirement of their "risk management" accreditation standard. Zero RCA (root-cause-analysis) workflow exists outside 2 narrow, module-specific `root_cause` text fields (infection control, OEM workplace incidents). | `grep` across `database-provisioning.service.ts`: only 4 near-miss/quality hits, all narrow-scope | HIGH |
| G2 | **No physician/clinical-staff credentialing & privileging system.** Nothing tracks medical staff licenses, scope-of-practice privileges, malpractice/indemnity cover, or CME/CPD compliance across the medical staff body. (The HIV Centre of Excellence's "Clinical Training Platform" is a CPD course/certificate system for a specific programme, not general medical-staff credentialing — different thing.) Every accreditation framework in this region requires proof that clinicians practising in a facility are currently licensed, privileged for the procedures they perform, and covered. | `grep -i "medical_staff\|physician_credential\|malpractice\|license_expiry"`: 0 hits | MEDIUM-HIGH |
| G3 | **No staff duty rostering / shift scheduling system.** Nurse task worklists exist (`nurse_tasks`), but nothing manages who is rostered on which ward, on-call schedules, or shift handover records at the facility-operations level — a real operational need for a hospital running 24/7 wards, not just a clinic. | `grep -i "duty_roster\|shift_schedule\|on_call_roster"`: 0 hits | MEDIUM |
| G4 | **No biomedical equipment maintenance/calibration tracking.** COHSASA's "technical services" standards cover equipment safety. Every `calibration` hit in the schema is unrelated (AI model calibration metrics) — there's no register of clinical equipment (ventilators, infusion pumps, defibrillators, monitors) with service/calibration due-dates and out-of-service flagging. | `grep -i "biomedical\|equipment_maintenance"`: 0 real hits (only AI-model calibration false positives) | MEDIUM |
| G5 | **Medical-aid integration is Zimbabwe-only** (AHFoZ, CBHI, NHIF) — correct for the current primary market, but a real gap if the roadmap includes expansion into South Africa (Discovery Health, Bonitas, Fedhealth, Momentum, GEMS) or other SADC countries with their own scheme ecosystems. The claims/eligibility architecture (per README's "Real-time medical-aid integration" work from the ZW Market-Fit roadmap) is presumably Zimbabwe-scheme-shaped rather than a pluggable multi-scheme framework — worth confirming before any regional expansion, not before. | README §"Zimbabwe Market-Fit Additions"; not independently re-verified in code this pass | LOW (contingent on expansion plans) |
| G6 | **No POPIA (South Africa) compliance layer** — Umoya has deep, genuinely excellent Zimbabwe CDPA 2021 compliance (18-control register, POTRAZ 72-hour breach notification, BAA vendor registry). South Africa's equivalent (Protection of Personal Information Act) has different specific requirements (Information Regulator notification timelines, different lawful-basis categories). Only relevant if/when expanding into the South African market specifically — flagging now so it's not forgotten later, not because it's needed today. | Not present; README's compliance section is entirely CDPA-framed | LOW (contingent on expansion plans) |

**Not flagged as a gap, but worth a closer look in a future session:** Umoya's ICU AI dashboard has "early deterioration alerts" per the README, but the audit didn't establish whether this is a genuinely predictive model (Netcare's flagship feature predicts decline up to 10 hours ahead) or a reactive threshold-crossing alert (e.g. "SpO₂ dropped below 90 now"). These are very different in clinical value and marketing terms for a hospital comparing Umoya to CareOn. Worth a targeted code read before claiming parity either way — not done here to avoid an unverified claim.

## Part 2 — Sprint Plan (S274+)

| Sprint | Title | What It Delivers | DB provisioning touch? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S274** | Patient Safety Incident Reporting & RCA | General-purpose incident register (event type, severity/harm level, patient/staff involved, immediate actions taken) covering falls, medication errors, wrong-site/wrong-patient events, specimen errors, and any other category — not module-specific. RCA workflow (five-whys or fishbone structure, contributing factors, corrective actions with owner/due-date, closure). Facility-wide incident dashboard with trend analysis by category/ward/severity — this is the single highest-value addition for COHSASA-style accreditation readiness. | Yes — new provisioning bundle, golden rule applies |
| **S275** | Clinical Staff Credentialing & Privileging | Medical staff register: license number + expiry, specialty/scope-of-practice privileges granted per facility, malpractice/indemnity cover expiry, CPD/CME compliance status. Expiry alerts (license/cover lapsing within 30/60/90 days) surfaced to admin. Privilege-to-procedure matching (optional stretch: flag if a clinician performs a procedure outside their granted privileges). | Yes |
| **S276** | Staff Duty Rostering | Ward-level shift roster (who's on which ward, which shift, on-call status), shift handover notes, and a simple conflict check (no clinician double-booked across two wards in an overlapping shift). Scope this modestly first — a full rostering optimizer is a much bigger project; start with the record-keeping and conflict-detection layer. | Yes |
| **S277** | Biomedical Equipment Register | Equipment inventory (ventilators, infusion pumps, defibrillators, monitors, etc.) with service/calibration due-dates, out-of-service flagging, and a maintenance history log. Surface overdue-calibration equipment on a facility dashboard — directly maps to a COHSASA technical-services standard. | Yes |
| **S278** | ICU Predictive Deterioration — Verify and Close the Gap | First: read the actual "early deterioration alerts" implementation in the ICU AI dashboard/service to establish whether it's already a real predictive model or a reactive threshold alert. If reactive, this is the one item worth building toward parity with the Netcare benchmark (ML-based lookahead deterioration prediction from vitals/labs trends) — but only after confirming the gap is real, not assumed. | No (unless the predictive model needs new feature-store tables) |
| **S279** *(deferred — expansion-contingent)* | Multi-Scheme Medical Aid Framework | Only worth scheduling once regional expansion beyond Zimbabwe is actually planned. Generalize the medical-aid claims/eligibility architecture from Zimbabwe-scheme-shaped to a pluggable adapter model, then add South African scheme adapters (Discovery Health, Bonitas, etc.) as the first real client. | Yes, when scheduled |
| **S280** *(deferred — expansion-contingent)* | POPIA Compliance Layer | Mirror the existing CDPA 2021 compliance architecture (control register, breach notification workflow, vendor registry) for South Africa's POPIA-specific requirements. Only worth scheduling alongside actual South African market entry. | Possibly, when scheduled |

S274–S278 are real, present-day gaps worth scheduling regardless of expansion plans — they matter for any private hospital in the current Zimbabwe market pursuing COHSASA accreditation, not just for regional growth. S279–S280 are flagged for awareness but intentionally deferred until expansion is a confirmed plan, not built speculatively ahead of need.
